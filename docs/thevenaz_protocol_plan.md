# Thévenaz protocol plan — put our estimator inside their published experiment

Written 2026-08-20. Serves item 1 of `docs/external_comparison_publication_plan.md`, which found that
of the twelve third-party engines in our comparison, TurboReg is the only one whose paper publishes a
reproducible accuracy protocol at all.

## Outcome

**A warping index for our estimators, measured inside Thévenaz, Rüttimann and Unser's own experimental
design, beside TurboReg measured the same way** — so the result can be read against a printed table
rather than only against our own benchmark. And, as the control that makes it mean anything, a
demonstration that our reproduction of their protocol returns TurboReg's *published* number when asked
their question in their transformation class.

If the harness cannot reproduce their number, we have misunderstood their protocol and nothing else in
this plan is worth reporting. That check comes first and it is the whole of Stage 0.

## Their experiment, as published

From Thévenaz, Rüttimann & Unser 1998, IEEE Transactions on Image Processing 7(1):27–41, section V.

1. Take one reference image. Theirs is an MRI slice, their Fig. 3.
2. Draw a random transformation: rotation uniform on ±5° (±π/36), and an independent uniform
   translation on ±2.5 pixels in each coordinate.
3. **Apply it twice, in opposite directions.** The inverse produces the test image; the forward
   produces the reference image. Their stated reason: with this construction neither algorithm being
   compared is favoured by the resampling. The transformation to be recovered is the square of the
   random one, so ±10° and ±5 pixels.
4. Resample with a **high-order spline model**, close to sinc — deliberately not the model being
   fitted, so an estimator is not being tested on data generated by its own image model.
5. Register test to reference and compare the recovered transformation to the true one with the
   **warping index**

   > ϖ = (1 / |V|) · Σ over pixels x in V of ‖ p(x) − p̃(x) ‖

   the mean over the region of interest of the distance between where the true transformation sends a
   pixel and where the estimated one does. Units are pixels.
6. **Pool 100 random transformations per algorithm**, using the same set of transformations for every
   algorithm compared.

They compare bilinear against bicubic interpolation and standard Marquardt–Levenberg against their
modified version — bilinear comes out about fivefold less accurate. **They never compare against
another author's method.**

## Why this is worth doing, in one paragraph

`docs/external_comparison_publication_plan.md` establishes that our per-frame error already *is* their
warping index: for a translation-only model the distance ‖p(x) − p̃(x)‖ is the same at every pixel, so
`FullSelectorFactorialBenchmark.controlledMetrics`, which computes `hypot(dx − true_dx, dy − true_dy)`,
computes exactly ϖ. We have been reporting their metric without saying so. What we have never done is
report it on **their** data design, and that is the difference between "our benchmark says we are
accurate" and "here is our number in the experiment the field already accepts".

## The limit that must be stated before anything is run

**Their headline number is for affine recovery. We cannot do affine at all.**

Their algorithm searches for a general affine transformation even though the truth is rigid — the
paper says so explicitly. Our estimators do not:

| | Transformation recovered |
|---|---|
| `AREA_CORRELATION`, and both its refinement variants | translation only |
| `LOG_RATIO_FIT` | translation; rotation available behind `PairAligner.Options.fitRotation`, **off by default and unproven — the field's comment reads "v0.1.0 is translation only"** |
| TurboReg | translation, rigid body, scaled rotation, affine, bilinear — selectable |

Fitting fewer parameters generally *improves* accuracy on a truth that lies inside the smaller class,
so quoting our translation-restricted number against their affine number would flatter us, and a
reviewer who knows the paper will catch it immediately.

**So the comparison is transformation-class-matched, not table-matched.** TurboReg is run restricted to
the same class we are. Their published affine figure appears once, in Stage 0, as a check on the
harness — never as a competitor to ours.

## Stage 0 — Reproduce their number before measuring ours

**Nothing about our estimators is run in this stage.** The point is to find out whether our
reconstruction of their protocol behaves like theirs.

1. **Read Table III off the printed paper.** The numeric cells are rasterised images in the PDF
   distributed by EPFL Infoscience, so they cannot be extracted from the text layer — somebody has to
   look at the page. Write the ML*3 warping index into `ThevenazProtocolBenchmark` as a constant,
   **with the tolerance, before any of our own numbers exist.**
2. Build the generator: random rigid transformation at their ranges, applied twice about the image
   centre, resampled with our highest-order spline model.
3. Run **TurboReg in affine mode**, 100 pooled transformations, and compute ϖ.
4. Compare against their published figure.

> **Exit gate.** Our ϖ for TurboReg-affine is within a factor of two of the published ML*3 value. A
> factor of two is deliberately loose: their machine, their image, their spline degree and their
> region of interest are all unknown to us at that precision, and the question being asked is whether
> we have built the same experiment, not whether we have built the same computer. **Outside a factor
> of two, stop.** Something in the protocol is not what we think it is, and the rest of the plan would
> be measuring that misunderstanding.

**The likeliest cause of a failure here is the region of interest.** ϖ is a mean over a region V that
the paper does not fully pin down; a mean over the whole frame and a mean over a central crop differ,
and rotation makes them differ more the further from the centre you go. If the gate fails, vary V
first, and record which choice reproduces the published value — that is itself a useful methodological
note for the write-up.

## Stage 1 — The harness

`src/test/java/logratio/ThevenazProtocolBenchmark.java`. New test-side code only.

- **The generator**, seeded and recorded, so the same 100 transformations go to every arm exactly as
  the paper requires. Write them to a CSV beside the results.
- **The warping index for a general transform** — the existing `controlledMetrics` shortcut is only
  valid for pure translation, and this harness must not silently reuse it. Implement ϖ as the paper
  defines it, over an explicit region of interest, and unit-test it against the translation case where
  the two must agree exactly.
- **Arms**: TurboReg at each transformation mode we care about, each of our estimators, and a
  do-nothing arm so a gross failure is still visible.
- **Reporting**: pooled mean ϖ as the paper reports, plus the median and the worst of the 100, because
  a pooled mean hides a single divergence and this project's own benchmark has been bitten by that
  repeatedly.

> **Exit gate.** The general ϖ implementation agrees with `controlledMetrics` to `0.000e+00` on a
> translation-only case. Both are computing the same quantity there; if they disagree, one is wrong.

## Stage 2 — Translation-restricted, which is the honest headline

TurboReg restricted to translation, against `LOG_RATIO_FIT`, `AREA_CORRELATION` and
`AREA_CORRELATION_NEWTON`, on the same 100 transformations, with the rotation component of the
generator **set to zero** — because a translation-only estimator asked to recover a rotated image is
being asked a question it cannot answer, and the resulting number would measure the question rather
than the estimator.

Run it on two bodies of material:

1. **One reference image**, as they did, so the design matches theirs exactly.
2. **All twenty development seed series**, one frame each. One image is a case study, not a
   measurement; twenty gives a distribution and costs nothing extra, since the harness is already
   built.

> **Exit gate.** No gate on who wins. This is a measurement, not a decision — nothing ships on it and
> nothing is tuned to it. The gate is on the evidence: 100 transformations per arm, the same 100 for
> every arm, the seed recorded, and the do-nothing arm behaving as expected.

## Stage 3 — Rigid body, only if Stage 2 is clean

Turning on `PairAligner.Options.fitRotation` exercises a code path that ships switched off and is
described in its own field comment as not part of v0.1.0. **Treat it as unproven code, not as a
setting.**

1. Unit-test rotation recovery on a synthetic rotation with a known answer, before any comparison.
2. Then run TurboReg in rigid-body mode against `LOG_RATIO_FIT` with rotation fitting on, at their full
   ±10° and ±5 px.

> **Exit gate before comparing.** Rotation recovery on the synthetic fixture is accurate to a stated
> tolerance and does not degrade the translation estimate relative to `fitRotation = false` on the
> zero-rotation case. If rotation fitting makes pure translation worse, that is a bug to record and
> the stage stops — a comparison run on a broken path tells you nothing about the method.

**Area correlation does not enter this stage.** It has no rotation path and would have to be given
one, which is a different plan.

## Stage 4 — The noise experiment, if it is wanted

The paper also varies signal-to-noise, quoting results down to 0 dB, and reports that convergence
takes appreciably longer as noise rises. Repeating that is cheap once Stages 1 and 2 exist, and it
would sit directly beside Fast4DReg's twelve-level noise sweep, making one figure comparable to two
different papers at once.

Optional. Do it only if the write-up needs it.

## Risks, named before the result is known

1. **TurboReg beats us in its own experiment.** This is the expected outcome on rotation and a real
   possibility on translation. `docs/spline_estimator_plan.md` already records a reproduced threefold
   per-image-type advantage to TurboReg's estimator under our solver and names "TurboReg is a different
   algorithm" as the only remaining explanation. **That is the finding, not a failure**, and the write-up
   must be able to say it. Anyone who cannot publish that result should not run this experiment.
2. **Their criterion is not gain-invariant and their experiment has no gain change.** Squared intensity
   difference on a spline model is exactly the method that should win on clean synthetic transforms of
   a static image, and exactly the method that should fail when the lamp drifts. Their protocol cannot
   see that, by construction. **A loss in Stage 2 is not a verdict on the plugin**, and the honest
   counterweight is `docs/spline_estimator_plan.md`'s gain-fade experiment, which is planned and still
   unrun. Consider running them together.
3. **The inverse crime.** Generating test data with the same image model an estimator fits flatters
   that estimator. The paper guards against it with a near-sinc resample and we must too — and we must
   check that the guard does not accidentally favour *us*, since our estimator's model is an
   interpolating cubic B-spline and so is TurboReg's.
4. **One image is not a benchmark.** Their design uses one slice. Stage 2's twenty-image extension is
   the answer, and the single-image run is kept only so the design matches the paper.

## What must not move

**No file under `src/main` changes in Stages 0 to 2.** This is a test-side harness measuring shipped
behaviour. Stage 3 touches `fitRotation` only through its existing public flag; if it turns out that
flag needs fixing, that is a separate change with its own before/after reproducibility gate, in the
shape `docs/newton_refinement_stage1_findings.md` established.

## Completion definition

Complete when:

- the harness reproduces TurboReg's published affine warping index within the declared tolerance, or
  the stage stopped and recorded why;
- the general warping index agrees with the existing per-frame metric at `0.000e+00` on translation;
- our estimators and TurboReg have a pooled warping index over the same 100 transformations, in a
  matched transformation class, on one image and on twenty;
- whoever won is written down plainly, including if it is not us;
- the write-up states that the paper's own headline is affine and ours is not, so nobody quotes the
  two against each other.
